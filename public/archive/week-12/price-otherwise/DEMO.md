# Price, Otherwise — class demo

## 3–4 minute route

1. Begin in **Relation** view. Frame the question: what relationships disappear when a price is shown as one isolated number?
2. Click **HPAI wave reaches laying flocks**. Point out the definition, `50.7 million` value, date range, federal sources, and the limitation that this does not calculate a retail-price effect.
3. Follow **reduces → Table-egg layer inventory**, then **constrains → Tight supply meets changing demand**. Emphasize that the verbs are qualified.
4. Switch to **Chronology** without closing the detail panel. The selected evidence remains visible.
5. Compare the terracotta New York wholesale line with the muted-blue U.S. city average retail line. Wholesale peaks in February; retail peaks in March. This is sequence, not proof of a fixed one-month causal lag.
6. Click **Contracts and pricing strategies mute movement**. Use its limitation to explain why the prototype avoids a retailer-margin claim.
7. Open **Index**, search `March`, and filter to `Retail`.
8. Open **Sources** and end on the evidence library: USDA ERS/AMS, BLS via FRED, and CRS.

## Key language

- Wholesale: New York monthly benchmark for large eggs, USDA ERS using AMS data.
- Retail: BLS U.S. city average for Grade A large eggs, not seasonally adjusted.
- The series differ in geography, market stage, product mix, and methodology.
- Use “coincides with,” “contributed to,” “may delay,” and “descriptive sequence.” Avoid “caused,” “proved,” or a calculated margin.

## Pre-class check

- Load desktop and narrow mobile widths.
- Click every header control and close each drawer with × and Escape.
- Select a record, switch views twice, and confirm selection remains.
- Open at least one source from each agency family.
