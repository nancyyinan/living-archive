    PROTOTYPE QUESTION

    How does a policy acquire meaning through what came before and what undid it?

    This interface compares two readings of the same archive. Chronology shows when a rule appeared. Relation shows what it replaced, expanded, restricted, or reversed.

    THE TEST COLLECTION

    Seventeen primary records trace U.S. policy affecting international students from 2016 to 2026: entry and screening, maintenance of status, modes of study, work after graduation, and court intervention.

    HOW TO READ THE GRAPH

    Node position is an argument. In Relation view, policies sit near the documents they amend or answer. In Chronology view, records occupy separate policy rows and never cover one another. Color describes this project's editorial reading of what a record does; it is not an official legal classification.

    NEXT TEST

    The international-student collection tests whether a nonlinear timeline can reveal regulatory reversals that a conventional date list makes difficult to see.
